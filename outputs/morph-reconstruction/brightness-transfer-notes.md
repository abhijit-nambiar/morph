# Brightness normalization experiment

We tested a simple, bounded per-photo adjustment before applying the shared polynomial mapping. It compares the median luminance of the held-out original with the median luminance of the representative original, then shifts the held-out image toward the representative. It does not inspect the held-out edited image when choosing the adjustment. This is a whole-frame heuristic: image composition and intentional lighting influence it, and the adjustment units are renderer parameters rather than calibrated camera exposure stops.

On the same eight proposed scenes and 46 eligible original/edit pairs as the representative-sensitivity study, there were 286 representative-to-held-out comparisons. Mean reduced-resolution RGB RMSE changed from 0.0648 without the heuristic to 0.0664 with it. Error decreased in 108 comparisons and increased in 178. The mean range across alternative representatives fell from 0.0558 to 0.0518, but the range fell for only 24 of 46 held-out photos. Scene-03's mean range fell from 0.1233 to 0.0668, while several other scenes worsened. The comparisons reuse photos, so they are not independent observations.

This is not strong enough to enable the heuristic automatically: reduced sensitivity in some scenes did not translate into overall improvement on this dataset. The scenes and pair alignment remain unverified, different edits may represent different intent, and pixel error is not a measure of aesthetic quality. These results describe one album at 128-pixel evaluation resolution; full-resolution, additional-album and human review are still needed.
